§ 01 — About

A practitioner moving toward theory

Statement of Interest

Growing up watching my father — a shrimp supplier — return with one recurring question: "When will the truck arrive, and what is the condition of the shrimp?" Those words captured the supply chain inefficiencies and poor logistics across Bangladesh that have stayed with me ever since, and committed me to tackling problems like transportation optimization, inventory control, and demand planning.

I am an Industrial and Production Engineering graduate from the Military Institute of Science and Technology (MIST), currently serving as a Supply Services Executive at Reckitt Benckiser Bangladesh PLC. Over three years I have managed international supply planning operations and worked with Reckitt's global team on the implementation of Kinaxis RapidResponse — enabling real-time supply chain visibility, AI-powered forecasting, and scenario-based planning.

That gap between what works in practice and what can be proven optimal is what draws me toward doctoral research. My interests sit at the intersection of mixed-integer and network optimization, stochastic and distributionally robust programming, and data-driven decision modeling — areas where the mathematical work has direct consequence for the kinds of supplier-selection, inventory, and routing problems I face professionally.

I am applying for 2027 admission to PhD programs in Industrial Engineering, Operations Research, and Systems Engineering, and I welcome conversation with faculty whose work intersects these areas.

§ 04 — Research Output

Published and archival work

Book Chapter
Springer · Joining Operations for Aerospace Materials · 2024
Laser Welding of Lightweight Alloys
A. Akther, N. Kabir, M. H. Takib, S. Rahman, M. E. Hoque
Co-authored chapter examining advanced laser welding techniques for lightweight alloys in automotive, aerospace, and biomedical contexts. Analyzed critical parameters — welding speed, laser power, and material reflectivity — and their interactions to optimize joint quality, with emphasis on precision, adaptability, economic feasibility, and sustainability of laser processes in industrial joining operations.
Undergraduate Thesis
Military Institute of Science and Technology · 2022
High-Speed Machining of Inconel 718 Applying Nanofluid-Based Minimum Quantity Lubrication
M. H. Takib — Sole Author
Designed and executed a Response Surface Methodology (RSM) study using Central Composite Design to optimize cutting parameters for a nickel-based superalloy under nanofluid-based MQL. Identified spindle speed, feed rate, and depth of cut as critical variables through ANOVA, then developed predictive models via RSM coupled with Genetic Algorithm (GA) in MATLAB to minimize surface roughness. Results validated through SEM and optical microscopy — an early exposure to optimization-under-constraints that motivated a pivot toward operations research.
