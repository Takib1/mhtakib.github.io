§ 01 — About

Process parameters decide everything

The lesson of my undergraduate research was simple and has stayed with me: in materials processing, the difference between a good outcome and a ruined one is rarely the material — it is the parameters. Spindle speed, feed rate, nanoparticle concentration, laser power. Small changes, large consequences, and far too many interacting variables to tune by intuition.

I am an Industrial and Production Engineering graduate from the Military Institute of Science and Technology (MIST), Bangladesh. My thesis investigated high-speed machining of Inconel 718 under nanofluid-based Minimum Quantity Lubrication (MQL) — hands-on work with a nickel superalloy and nanoparticle-laden cutting fluids, optimized computationally using Response Surface Methodology and Genetic Algorithms. Alongside it, I co-authored a Springer book chapter on laser welding of lightweight alloys, deepening my interest in metal processing and joining.

Professionally, I work at Reckitt Benckiser Bangladesh, where implementing the Kinaxis RapidResponse planning platform has sharpened the same habits research demands: building models, validating them against noisy real data, and trusting them only when they earn it.

I am applying for 2027 admission to PhD programs in advanced materials processing, manufacturing, and mechanical engineering — and I welcome conversation with faculty working at the intersection of experiment and computation.

§ 04 — Research Output

Published and archival work

Undergraduate Thesis
Military Institute of Science and Technology · 2022

High-Speed Machining of Inconel 718 Applying Nanofluid-Based Minimum Quantity Lubrication

M. H. Takib — Sole Author

Designed and executed a Response Surface Methodology study using Central Composite Design to optimize cutting parameters for a nickel-based superalloy under nanofluid-based MQL. Identified spindle speed, feed rate, and depth of cut as critical variables through ANOVA, then developed predictive models via RSM coupled with a Genetic Algorithm in MATLAB to minimize surface roughness. Machined surfaces were characterized by SEM and optical microscopy to validate the models — combining hands-on experience with nanoparticle-laden fluids and computational optimization of a demanding manufacturing process.

Book Chapter
Springer · Joining Operations for Aerospace Materials · 2024

Laser Welding of Lightweight Alloys

A. Akther, N. Kabir, M. H. Takib, S. Rahman, M. E. Hoque

Co-authored chapter examining advanced laser welding techniques for lightweight alloys in automotive, aerospace, and biomedical contexts. Analyzed critical parameters — welding speed, laser power, and material reflectivity — and their interactions to optimize joint quality, with emphasis on precision, adaptability, economic feasibility, and sustainability of laser processes in industrial joining operations.
